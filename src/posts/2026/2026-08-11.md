## Hanoi to Vu Linh

I didn't sleep well the night before I began my scooter drive across Vietnam. 

I'm not one for planning much when I travel, and yet for this scooter trip I'd learned as much as I could. Dozens of blogs read, videos watched. Nevertheless I was nervous, very nervous.

My "experience" up until that point was as follows:
- 20 minutes on a scooter in Ibiza in 2018
- 5 years driving *a car* on the "wrong-side" of the road in New Zealand

So when I woke up (late) on Thursday, I wasn't pumped for the adventure, in fact I'd booked an extra night in Hanoi so I could drive the scooter back to the hotel before kicking off in earnest the next day.

### The itch

And yet as I was purchasing a raincoat for the journey, I started getting a buzz inside me, the feeling told me it was desperate to leave *now*.  I checked online how long the drive should take "~4 hours", and said to myself ok, I'll be a bit behind schedule, but I should be there about 5pm (~1.5 hours before sunset).

I jumped on the back of a scooter taxi and raced back to the hotel. Told them I wanted to check back early, grabbed my bag, and made my way to the rental place.

The staff at Tigit Motorbikes were helpful and got me underway pretty quickly after a couple of test drives (the second of which being the only time I nearly hit a parked car...), and I put my destination into my phone, *Vu Linh Palm House*.

### Some pain, some beauty

Getting out of the city was a mission... in addition to learning how to drive a scooter, I also hadn't factored in that my phone would overheat easily if I used Google Maps while driving (it being about 40c out). This meant at a point where I was amongst some of the most congested traffic, with multiple directions to go, I had to guess and second-guess my directions.

Fortunately my phone came back to life after some time in the shade, and I managed to get out onto some of the more outer-city roads. It was remarkable how quickly the drive transitioned from urban to rural. 

An hour behind schedule I was finally making progress, admiring the beautiful *Red River* as I drove.

It was amazing driving through tiny towns and villages during the late afternoon, seeing all the parents and grand-parents picking up their kids from school, and go about their afternoon activities. 

### A lot more pain...

Perhaps I'd been watching the surroundings too much, because I'd managed to make my way to a freeway where scooters weren't allowed. 

Another setback.

This setback added another hour to my journey. 

A setback which would now see me arriving at my intended accommodation at least half an hour after sunset... 

One of the key pieces of advice consistently communicated by others was to never drive at night-time. 

My options were either find somewhere on the way to my destination, or crack-on. The issue with stopping sooner was the added time it would add to my next day... a theme which would become familiar throughout my trip. 

I cracked on, and entered the more rural, wooded landscapes north-west of Hanoi. 

### Racing the light

It was a beautiful drive, especially as the sun lowered, the sunset colours painted the mountains and forests beautifully, but there was dread for me in that beauty. 

Sunset was scheduled for 6:30pm, and yet as 6:30pm came by I still perceived a good amount of light, I was pleased, my phone told me I should arrive 7:05pm, not too bad I thought at this rate.

Unfortunately the sun raced to the other side of the planet, and stole my vision away.. from about 6:50pm it was pitch black, with another 15 minutes or so to go. Only a couple of kilometres but in total darkness, on an uneven road.

My scooter light helped as much as it could but my pace dropped drastically. After what felt like eternity between the final two villages, I found my homestay. 

### Relief

The homestay family were incredible, despite arriving into total darkness they welcomed me straight into their outdoor patio where they'd setup many tables for hosting guests. It looked like they could host about 50 people at capacity, but that night it was me, a Canadian, and American, and their two motorbike tour guides. 

Within minutes of arrival I'd been taken to my room to quickly refresh and join them for dinner. 

A wonderful array of dishes was presented including pork, chicken, and fish, as well as dozens of small vegetarian dishes. With my anxiety waning, and intoxication rising, I relaxed into the evening with good company, and great food. 

I slept early, and I slept for a long time.

### Notes

A big thank you to Dai & his family for hosting me that evening. I can't recommend highly enough others that do the trip go and visit them at their wonderful home in Vu Linh.
